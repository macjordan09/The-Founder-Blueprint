# Customer Research: Target Audiences

This document lists initial audience groups for the project, describes their needs and use cases, and ranks them by priority for early outreach, validation, and product development.

Audience groups (descriptions & use cases)

1. Investors
   - Description: Early-stage and growth investors evaluating deals or sectors in African markets.
   - Primary needs: fast, source-tagged diligence; comparable country/sector briefs; data to de-risk allocation decisions.
   - Use cases: pre-read for investment committees, rapid sector scans, sourcing deal leads.

2. Founders
   - Description: Local and international founders building products or expanding in African markets.
   - Primary needs: market validation, competitor landscape, go-to-market playbooks, partner discovery.
   - Use cases: product-market fit research, fundraising preparation, partner diligence.

3. African diaspora professionals
   - Description: Diaspora executives, operators, and professionals with experience or ties to African markets.
   - Primary needs: trustworthy summaries, ways to surface/reuse local knowledge, ways to contribute provenance.
   - Use cases: advising founders, sourcing deals, validation of local context, facilitation of introductions.

4. Corporate executives
   - Description: BD, strategy, and partnerships teams at firms exploring market entry or partnerships in Africa.
   - Primary needs: country/sector risk assessments, partner vetting frameworks, regulation and compliance summaries.
   - Use cases: market entry scoping, partner diligence, strategic planning.

5. African companies exploring global expansion
   - Description: High-growth African firms seeking entry into new international markets.
   - Primary needs: competitor & partner landscape in target markets, compliance and market-entry checklists, partner discovery.
   - Use cases: international expansion planning, partner selection, investor readiness materials.

6. Canadian companies exploring Africa
   - Description: Canadian corporates and SMEs targeting Africa (explicit project audience).
   - Primary needs: country briefings that surface regulatory differences, local partners, and market channels relevant to Canadian businesses.
   - Use cases: go-to-market strategy, local partner sourcing, compliance checks.

7. Development organizations
   - Description: Multilaterals, foundations, and NGOs that design programs and fund interventions in African contexts.
   - Primary needs: evidence-based country/sector analyses, outcome measurement, local partner discovery.
   - Use cases: program design, monitoring & evaluation, due diligence for grantees.

8. Policy makers
   - Description: Government or public-sector decision-makers (domestic and foreign) designing policy or bilateral programs.
   - Primary needs: timely, impartial intelligence on economic conditions, regulatory shifts, and social outcomes.
   - Use cases: policy formulation, cross-country comparisons, regulatory forecasting.

9. Researchers
   - Description: Academics and think-tank researchers studying African markets, policy, and development.
   - Primary needs: well-documented datasets, reproducible methodology, reference briefs.
   - Use cases: secondary analysis, citation, collaborative research.

10. Journalists
   - Description: Reporters and editors covering Africa-focused business, policy, and tech beats.
   - Primary needs: reliable facts, source chains, quick contextual briefs for stories.
   - Use cases: story research, fact-checking, lead generation for deeper investigation.

Priority ranking (highest → lowest) with short rationale

1. Investors — High willingness to pay, immediate need for de-risking investments, and can introduce pilot deals for validation.
2. Founders — Frequent, recurring users; product-market fit research directly informs product and content priorities.
3. African diaspora professionals — High trust capital and domain knowledge; early contributors and amplifiers of provenance and context.
4. Corporate executives — Larger budgets and clearly defined procurement cycles; longer sales cycles but high impact.
5. African companies exploring global expansion — Practical users who will use playbooks and partner discovery tools.
6. Canadian companies exploring Africa — A useful geographic segmentation with specific regulatory and market needs.
7. Development organizations — Strong use case for evidence, potential funders/partners, but slower procurement and specific reporting needs.
8. Policy makers — High impact but slow-moving; often require neutral, rigorous sourcing and formal procurement.
9. Researchers — Important for credibility and methodologies; more likely to consume than pay.
10. Journalists — Valuable for amplification and short-term usage but lower strategic priority for early product development.

Recommended early targets and why

- Primary cohort: Investors + Founders + African diaspora professionals
  - Why: these groups provide high-frequency usage, credible feedback, domain knowledge, and potential funding or referrals.
  - Early validation actions: run 10 interviews across these groups; build 3 sample briefs and run them through real diligence or product discovery workflows.

- Secondary cohort (next 3 months): Corporate executives + African & Canadian companies
  - Why: these groups validate product-market fit for commercial GTM; can reveal procurement and enterprise requirements.

- Later / strategic collaborators: Development orgs, policy makers, researchers, journalists
  - Why: partnerships for datasets, credibility, and scale — pursue once core product-market fit is established.

Suggested validation metrics per audience

- Investors: time-to-decision, percent citing improved confidence in diligence, pilot deal conversions triggered by an artifact.
- Founders: time-to-first-customer or time-to-MVP milestones shortened, reuse of playbooks, experiment completion rate.
- Diaspora professionals: number of contributions (leads, annotations), referral rate to other decision-makers.
- Corporate / company users: pilot engagements, procurement signals, average contract value (if commercialized).
- Development / policy / research: data citations, partnership agreements, usage in program design.
