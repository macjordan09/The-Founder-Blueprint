# Central Problem

One-line

Decision-makers who want to invest in, build in, partner with, or understand Africa lack trusted, structured, evidence-based intelligence to make confident, timely decisions.

Expanded problem statement

Investors, corporate partners, program leaders, and operators focused on African markets face fragmented sources, inconsistent methodologies, and opaque data—making it hard to separate signal from noise. Without trusted, structured, and up-to-date intelligence, these decision-makers delay or misdirect capital and resources, increase risk, and repeat avoidable mistakes.

Who this affects (priority personas)

- Early-stage and growth investors evaluating deals or sectors in African markets
- Corporate BD and partnerships teams exploring market entry or local alliances
- Operators and founders seeking product-market fit, fundraising, or partnerships across African countries
- Policy-makers, program officers, and incubators designing interventions or grant programs

Primary pain points

- Fragmentation: intelligence is scattered across slide decks, ad-hoc reports, gated consulting outputs, and informal local knowledge.
- Trust & provenance: verifying the source, methodology, and recency of claims is costly and time-consuming.
- Lack of structure: inconsistent frameworks make cross-market comparisons and prioritization difficult.
- Timeliness: rapidly changing regulations, macro conditions, or competitor moves are not tracked centrally.
- Usability: insights are rarely packaged for the decision workflow (investment memo, partner diligence checklist, go-to-market plan).

Root causes

- Limited centralized data infrastructure and standardized measurement across markets and sectors.
- Over-reliance on bespoke, non-reproducible local knowledge held by individuals.
- Incentives for vendors to produce bespoke narratives rather than reusable, versioned data artifacts.
- Barriers to open data and interoperability between sources.

Consequences / cost of the problem

- Slower deal flow and missed opportunities as teams defer decisions.
- Misallocated capital or partnerships based on incomplete signals.
- Repeated due-diligence costs and duplicated work across teams and organizations.
- Lower trust in local partners and underinvestment in high-potential markets.

What success looks like (desired outcome)

- Decision-makers can access structured, source-tagged, and comparable intelligence that fits their workflow (country/sector briefs, checklists, datasets).
- Faster, better decisions with measurable reductions in time-to-decision and due-diligence cost.
- Local knowledge is captured, versioned, and reused, reducing single-person dependencies and increasing transparency.

Concrete metrics to track

- Median time-to-decision for target users
- Self-reported percent reduction in duplicated diligence tasks
- Active users among target decision-makers (monthly)
- Trust score: percent of users rating intelligence as "trusted" or "highly reliable"
- Count of repeatable, versioned intelligence artifacts (country briefs, sector playbooks, datasets)

Working hypotheses

- H1: Standardized, source-tagged market briefs and playbooks will reduce time-to-decision by >=25% in an initial pilot cohort.
- H2: Intelligence artifacts that include verifiable datasets and reproducible methodology will score higher on trust and adoption than ad-hoc sources.
- H3: Packaging intelligence into decision templates (investment checklist, partner rubric) will increase actionable outcomes (term sheets, signed partnerships) from pilot users.

Validation plan (minimal steps)

1. Stakeholder interviews: speak with 8–12 decision-makers across investors, corporates, and founders to confirm the pain, decision flow, and preferred artifact formats.
2. Prototype artifacts: produce 3 short, source-tagged market briefs (example: one country brief, one sector brief, one partnership checklist) and test them in real workflows with 3 pilot users.
3. Measurement: track time-to-decision, artifact reuse, and trust rating throughout a 6–8 week pilot.
4. Iterate templates and data sources based on feedback; prioritize building a library of versioned intelligence artifacts.

Suggested interview questions

- Walk me through the last time you evaluated an opportunity in Africa — what information did you need and where did you get it?
- What caused the most friction or uncertainty in that process?
- Which artifacts (country brief, dataset, partnership checklist) would have changed your decision or speeded it up?
- How do you currently verify claims or data about market opportunity or partners?
- What format and cadence of intelligence would be most useful to your team?
