# Editorial Standards

Purpose

This document sets the editorial rules and expectations for all public-facing intelligence: newsletters, briefs, articles, and signal items. The standards exist to build trust, ensure reproducibility, and make every piece of work useful for real decisions.

Core rules (enforced)

- No hype. Avoid sensational language and clickbait framing. Signal is valuable; drama is not.
- No gossip. Do not publish unverified rumors, hearsay, or private conversations presented as fact.
- No unsupported claims. Every major claim must include at least one verifiable source; preference for primary or high-quality secondary sources.
- Separate opinion from fact. Label analysis and conjecture explicitly ("Analysis" / "Opinion") and keep them distinct from sourced facts.
- Decision-first writing. Every article, brief, or signal item must make at least one practical recommendation or clearly state how it helps a decision (investment, partnership, entry, policy).

Claim & sourcing policy

- "Major claim" = any statement that would materially change a decision (e.g., market size, regulatory change, major funding round, partnership). Major claims must cite primary sources (filings, public reports, datasets, official statements) or two independent reputable secondary sources.
- For metrics and figures, include the original dataset or report URL, publication date, and a short note on method (e.g., sampling, coverage limits).
- Use inline links and an explicit "Sources" section at the bottom with full citations.

Opinion vs fact

- Facts: data, events, quotes, reports — must be source-linked.
- Analysis / Opinion: labeled sections that interpret facts; may include hypotheses, recommended actions, or educated judgment. Opinions should include the author and date.

Structure required for every published piece

- Title (clear, non-sensational)
- TL;DR (1–2 sentences: key takeaway + recommended action)
- What happened / Key facts (sourced)
- Analysis / implications (clearly labeled opinion)
- Sources (full links + short provenance notes)
- Caveats & uncertainty (what we don’t know and how confident we are)
- Author & contributors (names, roles, and any COIs)
- Version & date

Editor checklist (must be completed before publish)

- [ ] All major claims have sources (>=1 primary or >=2 independent secondary).
- [ ] Data sources are accessible and linked (or clearly labelled "private/payed source").
- [ ] Opinion is labeled and not mixed into factual sections.
- [ ] The piece includes a clear decision ask or actionable implication.
- [ ] Sources and dates are present; provenance notes added for critical claims.
- [ ] At least one peer review (another researcher/editor) has sanity-checked the sources.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest disclosed.

Roles & responsibilities

- Author/Researcher: compiles sources, writes draft, documents methodology.
- Editor: confirms sourcing, clarity, and decision-focused framing; completes editor checklist.
- Fact-checker (if separate): verifies primary sources and data integrity.
- Contributor: local experts or diaspora contributors who provide context or annotations — must list provenance for any claims they add.

Versioning & corrections

- Publish with a version and date in the header.
- Corrections must be recorded at the bottom with date and short note describing the change.
- For material errors that change recommendations, publish a correction notice and send to subscribers who received the earlier version.

Enforcement

- Violations of these standards (e.g., publishing unverified claims, mixing opinion into facts) will trigger review, correction, and a short remediation note on process improvements.
