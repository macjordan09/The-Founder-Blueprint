# Fact Checking

Purpose

Define the end-to-end fact-checking workflow to ensure published claims are accurate, verifiable, and appropriately caveated.

Fact-checking stages

1. Source validation (pre-draft)
   - Confirm the original source exists and is accessible (link works; document snapshot if behind paywall).
   - Check the authority of the source (official agency, filing, dataset owner) and note any potential bias.

2. Triangulation (research stage)
   - Corroborate the claim with at least one independent source when possible.
   - If triangulation is not possible, explicitly label the claim as "single-source" and describe the risk.

3. Data validation (analysis stage)
   - Re-run key calculations from raw data where feasible.
   - Check units, dates, and denominators (e.g., per-capita vs absolute numbers).
   - Spot-check for transcription errors and off-by-one date ranges.

4. Source attribution & provenance (editing stage)
   - Ensure each claim has an inline link and a full citation in the Sources section.
   - For interview-derived claims, include interviewer, date, and consent/attribution status.

5. Final review (pre-publish)
   - Editor or designated fact-checker signs off on the editor checklist (see Editorial Standards).
   - For high-impact or novel claims, seek external review from a subject-matter expert.

Post-publication checks & corrections

- Monitor feedback channels for reported errors.
- If an error is found, update the article with a correction note describing the change, date, and reason.
- For material corrections affecting recommendations, send an update to subscribers and stakeholders who received the earlier version.

Correction policy (short)

- Minor errors (typos, formatting): fix inline and log in a maintenance changelog.
- Source/data errors that do not change the recommendation: update the source and add a correction note.
- Material errors that change recommendations or conclusions: publish a correction notice at the top, notify subscribers, and log the incident with root-cause and remediation steps.

Escalation & disputes

- If the author disputes a fact-check requirement, escalate to the editorial lead for arbitration.
- If an external party disputes published facts, request the challenger provide primary source evidence; if valid, publish correction and note the source.

Tools & artifacts

- Source registry: a simple spreadsheet or Notion table to track source URLs, snapshots, license, and date accessed.
- Fact-check checklist: attach the editorial checklist to every draft and record the sign-off.
- Snapshots: save critical documents or pages (PDF or HTML snapshot) and link them in the source registry.

Roles

- Fact-checker: verifies primary sources, reproduces calculations, and confirms attribution.
- Editor: ensures that the piece is decision-focused, that sources are present, and that corrections are handled following policy.

Example: verifying a funding round

- Primary source: company press release or regulatory filing.
- Secondary corroboration: coverage from two reputable outlets or a disclosure in a fund's portfolio.
- Data check: confirm amount, date, and participants across sources; confirm currency and convert if needed (state FX rate used).
